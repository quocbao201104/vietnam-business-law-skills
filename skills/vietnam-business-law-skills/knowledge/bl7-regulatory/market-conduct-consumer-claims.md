# BL7 — Market Conduct / Consumer / Claims / Competition

## Owns

Determining whether the way a business presents, sells, distributes, prices, communicates, contracts with, or competes around a product/service creates public-law market-conduct consequences, including consumer protection, advertising/claims, e-commerce selling conduct, unfair/deceptive practices, and competition/market-conduct propositions.

This unit owns **market-conduct propositions** after the relevant regulated role/perimeter is sufficiently resolved. It does not decide product/service operating permission, private contract formation, or privacy/data legality merely because the same customer interaction is involved.

## Does not own

- regulatory-perimeter/regulated-role discovery when unresolved — `regulatory-perimeter-role.md`;
- permission/license/product-market-entry state — `permission-entry-ongoing-compliance.md`;
- privacy/data processing legality — `data-privacy-digital-operations.md`;
- private contract formation/content/interpretation — BL3;
- breach/remedies/dispute procedure — BL4;
- tax — BL5;
- employment — BL6;
- foreign-investment/trade/customs — BL8.

## Activate when

Use when the decision depends on:

- advertising, marketing claims, endorsements, comparisons, disclosures, pricing presentation, promotions, or sales messaging;
- consumer/customer information duties, unfair/deceptive practice, cancellation/refund/after-sales conduct, standard-facing business practices, or mandatory customer protections;
- marketplace/platform selling conduct or digital sales practices apart from privacy/data processing;
- exclusivity, resale restrictions, bundling/tying, discrimination, coordination, information exchange, market power, competitor/customer restrictions, or another competition concern;
- whether a private contractual practice creates a separate public-law market-conduct problem;
- whether market conduct remains lawful after business role/channel/customer facts change.

Skip when the user only asks whether the business has permission to operate or whether a data-processing activity is lawful.

## Required state

Where material, consume:

- committed BL7 perimeter/regulated role;
- product/service and channel facts;
- customer type and transaction journey;
- actual advertising/claim/promotion/pricing/disclosure content;
- evidence supporting factual claims;
- contract/policy terms from BL3 as private-law content only;
- platform/intermediary role where material;
- market structure/position/counterparty facts for competition propositions;
- current/historical public-law authority;
- relevant action/communication date.

A claim or practice must be analyzed as used in context. Do not infer legality from isolated words or from the existence of a signed contract.

## Core distinctions

### Product/service may be lawful ≠ every claim is lawful

Permission to sell or operate does not establish that every advertising, comparative, efficacy, pricing, promotional, endorsement, or disclosure claim is permitted or adequately substantiated.

### Private agreement ≠ public-law conduct safe

A customer, distributor, platform, competitor, or supplier may agree to a term. Agreement does not establish that the conduct is consumer-safe, competition-safe, or otherwise permitted by mandatory market rules.

### Claim wording ≠ claim support

A statement can be factually phrased yet unsupported. Keep separate:

```text
claim content
claim meaning in context
required support
available evidence
public-law consequence
```

Do not invent evidence to preserve a preferred marketing claim.

### Disclosure ≠ cure everything

A disclaimer or disclosure may matter, but it does not automatically cure a misleading core claim, prohibited practice, invalid mandatory waiver, or competition problem.

### B2B ≠ competition-safe

The absence of a consumer relationship does not remove competition, advertising, sector, platform, or other market-conduct rules.

### Small business ≠ competition-exempt

Company size alone does not prove a competition proposition is irrelevant. Apply the actual materiality and current legal tests.

### Commercial preference ≠ legal justification

A preferred pricing, channel, exclusivity, bundling, ranking, review, seller-control, or competitor strategy must not drive a fabricated legal characterization.

### Customer remedy ≠ BL7 dispute remedy

BL7 owns mandatory market-conduct/consumer obligations and regulatory status. BL4 owns dispute posture/remedy/procedure when a concrete claim/dispute is pursued, except where a regulator-facing enforcement proposition remains public-law BL7 territory.

## Decision procedure

1. **State the conduct proposition.** Example: `May Business B make Claim C to Customer Group G in Channel H?`
2. **Consume perimeter/role state.** Do not reconstruct the regulatory role unless material evidence contradicts it.
3. **Characterize the actual conduct.** Claim, disclosure, pricing/promotion, customer practice, platform conduct, vertical restriction, competitor coordination, market-power conduct, or another concrete behavior.
4. **Identify protected audience/market relationship.** Consumer/customer/competitor/supplier/platform participant and context.
5. **Separate private-law content from public-law consequence.** BL3 supplies terms; BL7 tests mandatory conduct constraints.
6. **Resolve evidence/support needs.** Identify what factual claims require substantiation and what evidence actually exists.
7. **Resolve current authority.** Use the relevant communication/conduct date and current/historical regime.
8. **Commit conduct state.** Supported, conditional, verification-required, blocked/prohibited, disputed, or unresolved.
9. **Link the exact action.** `PUBLISH CLAIM`, `RUN PROMOTION`, `USE TERM`, `IMPOSE RESTRICTION`, etc. Readiness is action-specific.
10. **Route privacy/permission/dispute consequences outward.** Do not absorb sibling or BL4 propositions.

## Evidence requirements

Potential evidence includes:

- actual ads, landing pages, labels, scripts, listings, screenshots and promotion terms;
- substantiation/test/certification records for factual claims;
- price history/promotion calculations where material;
- customer terms/disclosures from BL3;
- complaint/refund/after-sales process evidence;
- platform rules as evidence of platform arrangements, not statutory authority;
- agreements and communications relevant to competition conduct;
- market-share/market-definition/economic facts where legally material;
- regulator guidance only at its proper authority level;
- binding/current primary authority for the legal proposition.

Do not treat internal marketing copy, platform UI, competitor behavior, or industry custom as proof that a practice is lawful.

## Live authority triggers

Use Authority Resolver for current consumer scope, mandatory disclosures/rights, prohibited/unfair practices, advertising/claim restrictions, promotion/pricing conditions, e-commerce selling duties, competition tests/thresholds/exemptions, regulator powers, and historical rules at the conduct date.

Do not hardcode current thresholds, mandatory wording, promotion caps, disclosure fields, consumer periods, competition presumptions, forms, or article numbers.

## Cross-track handoffs

### From BL3

Consume contract/term content. BL7 decides whether mandatory market-conduct rules constrain that content or its use. Do not rewrite BL3's contract proposition.

### From permission unit

Consume permission/activity state when conduct depends on what may be offered. Permission to operate does not prove the specific conduct is permitted.

### To BL4

If the matter becomes a concrete customer/competitor/private dispute, return committed conduct propositions to BL4 for remedy/procedure ownership. BL7 retains public-law regulatory/enforcement propositions.

### To data/privacy unit

If targeting, profiling, tracking, reviews, personalization, messaging, or customer operations create data-processing questions, route those propositions to `data-privacy-digital-operations.md`.

### To specialists

Competition or sector-specific specialist depth may be invoked only when the proposition materially requires it. Specialist output returns to BL7 owner review.

## Failure modes

- legal product treated as permission for any claim;
- signed customer/distributor agreement treated as mandatory-law waiver;
- disclaimer treated as universal cure;
- claim repeated from supplier/competitor without substantiation analysis;
- B2B treated as competition-exempt;
- small company treated as automatically outside competition law;
- market share/threshold recalled from memory;
- platform policy treated as statutory authority;
- consumer issue converted directly into BL4 without first resolving the mandatory conduct proposition;
- privacy/data issue silently decided inside market-conduct reasoning;
- loading all consumer/advertising/competition domains merely because the business sells something.

## Escalation

Increase verification for health/safety/financial or other high-impact claims, mass promotions, vulnerable consumers, regulated-product advertising, dominant/concentrated markets, exclusivity/bundling/coordination, platform ranking/control, repeated consumer complaints, or any conduct whose publication/execution is difficult to reverse.
