# Citation Guidance v0.2

Citation is evidence for a proposition, not decoration.

## Cite when

A material conclusion depends on current/historical legal authority, including rights, obligations, validity, remedy, tax treatment, threshold/rate, deadline, licensing, sanction, procedure, or current compliance.

## Do not impose a source quota

Use the **minimum sufficient authority set** for the proposition.

Sometimes one controlling provision is enough. Sometimes the correct rule requires a coordinated set, for example:

- base law;
- amending law;
- implementing decree;
- transition rule;
- authoritative interpretation where material.

Do not optimize for either citation count or single-source simplicity.

## Traceability

A material proposition should be traceable to:

- `proposition_id`;
- source identity/provenance;
- relevant provision/passage;
- authority/legal-force type;
- lifecycle/effective period;
- proposition-specific temporal anchor(s);
- `verified_at` / freshness where material;
- case-applicability conclusion owned by the responsible BL track.

## Separate four things

Do not collapse:

```text
source provenance
legal force
lifecycle status
case applicability
```

An official website can host non-binding guidance. A currently binding instrument can still be inapplicable to the transaction because of scope or transition rules.

## Separate authority from interpretation

Do not make an interpretive conclusion look like quoted law.

Make clear when a proposition is:

- source text/rule;
- owner decision about applicability;
- application to facts;
- derived legal interpretation;
- business recommendation.

## User/document facts

Do not cite externally for facts supplied by the user unless independently verifying them is itself material.

A statement in an uploaded document is evidence/representation, not automatically a resolved factual proposition.
